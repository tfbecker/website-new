I had a splendid time. The weather was impeccable. It was so energizing to meet acquaintances and friends I haven't seen in a long time. It was absolutely buzzing.

My observations after five days there.

They also just cook with water. The talent is great, but not because individual talent is crazy. I only have two explanations for this. Either I think very highly of myself and my technical abilities, or it is more about the density of talent than about complete outlier talent.

I'm definitely jealous that people are moving there. But my realization is that I've seen an absolutely biased group of people: very hardworking, top 0.1% Germans moving to SF and actually getting funding. So yes, one can be jealous of the top 1% founders who already exited and are now moving to SF.

I met the cream of the crop. You do a master's at Stanford, you get into YC, it doesn't work out, you pivot, you switch co-founders, and then you finally link up with your buddy who already exited a company in Germany and raised money independently. You double the valuation of your company without any dilution because investors are so keen to back you from both sides, from SF and from Berlin.

Aside from the profiles I met during my time in VC, which were just very flashy, this is still pretty damn good talent. Even at random dinners. Everyone is a founder, everyone is building hardware these days.

I always thought the single market in America is helpful for building. Now I definitely know it is. I met someone doing autonomous refueling for drones, which seems to me like such a niche problem. But if you have the American defense apparatus behind you, with an Iran war and big airports, even a refuel truck can be a venture case.

Another observation: everyone is fucking rich. Everyday things are not that expensive in my opinion, going to Trader Joe's is okay. I'll see the difference to New York price-wise because I just landed here. Housing is crazy pricey from what I've heard. But everyone is wealthy. I was invited to a dinner as a plus one, didn't even know the host, and he cooked fucking steak and tuna. This guy must have spent five, six hundred dollars on ingredients alone, which is absolutely bonkers to me. In Germany you chip in 20 or 30 euros for a dinner and you eat spaghetti with pesto, for lack of a better example.

One friend of mine and his co-founder just landed there and they are racing around. An absolutely crazy ambitious hardware company going after Meta. I'm good friends with one of them and I'm just jealous. They make everything seem ballsy, and you think you're wasting your life away building something not that ambitious. To be realistic though: how would your life look if you worked at a fucking tier three corporate tech company that isn't growing that much anymore? Look at Bending Spoons acquiring all of these boring-ass companies. But I guess you can always switch.

There are definitely ups and downs, too. I met a friend who got funding and has now closed down his shop, after moving to New York and then to SF to be closer to where he can sell. But selling is not that easy. Even if you have an open source project with 600,000 downloads, it's really hard to monetize.

It's not imposter syndrome, but sometimes you feel like a failure next to all of these ambitious and accomplished people, even though it's only paper valuations. I alternated between being anxious about my life, my trajectory and my plan, and being completely energized by the caliber of people. I think that's how I would summarize my impression.
